# Red Light Camera Impact Analysis — Toronto (2006–2021)

## Overview
This project analyzes the impact of **red light cameras (RLCs)** on traffic accidents in the City of Toronto between **2006 and 2021**. The analysis focuses on whether RLC installations are associated with changes in accident rates, alongside other contributing factors such as traffic volume, seasonality, weather, speed, and alcohol.

## Data Sources
The analysis uses multiple datasets from the **City of Toronto Open Data Portal**, including:
- Intersection characteristics
- Red light camera locations and activation dates
- Traffic volume data
- Motor vehicle collisions involving killed or seriously injured persons

## Key Questions
- Do red light cameras reduce serious traffic accidents at intersections?
- How does traffic volume relate to accident frequency?
- What other factors (season, weather, speed, alcohol) influence accident rates?

## Methods
- Data cleaning and merging of multiple public datasets
- Feature engineering to align accident data with intersections
- Exploratory data analysis and visualizations
- Descriptive statistics and correlation analysis

## Key Findings
- The overall impact of red light cameras on accident rates is **inconclusive** at a city-wide level.
- **Traffic volume** shows a moderate positive correlation with accident frequency.
- **Seasonality** is a strong factor, with higher accident rates during summer months.
- Speeding has a stronger association with accidents than alcohol.

## Tools
Python, pandas, NumPy, matplotlib, seaborn, Jupyter Notebook
